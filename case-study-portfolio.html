Case study · 2026 · Personal site

This portfolio, spec-first

Role: product owner & QA · Stack: HTML, CSS, JavaScript

Problem

As a second-year CSE student, I needed a professional web presence for recruiters and potential clients — but I had no shipped projects yet. The common answer is to pad a portfolio with invented work. I decided the site itself would be the first project, built to a written spec, with a hard rule: nothing fabricated.

Target user

Recruiters screening internship candidates, and founders or clients considering a small project. Both skim fast, read on phones, and are allergic to inflated claims.

My role

I wrote the full product brief and acted as owner and reviewer: defining the goal, audience, required sections, technical constraints (responsive layout, reduced-motion support, semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, SEO and social metadata) and content rules, then reviewing the build against that brief. Implementation was done with AI assistance under my direction.

Process

  • Wrote a detailed spec first: sections, states, accessibility, and explicit "do not" rules (no fake testimonials, stats, or projects).
  • Chose plain HTML/CSS/JS over Next.js — no build step, free hosting, and content anyone can edit in a text editor.
  • Defined a small design system: two type families, one accent colour, hairline dividers, a single spacing scale.
  • Reviewed the result against the brief: link checks, mobile layout, keyboard navigation, honest copy.

Key decisions

  • Honesty as a feature. An empty-but-truthful projects section, with a visible note explaining it, instead of filler.
  • Simplest stack that works. Static files beat a framework when there's nothing dynamic to render.
  • Editorial, not "AI-modern". No gradients, no glowing cards — serif headlines, monospace labels, warm paper background.
  • Motion is optional. Scroll reveals are skipped entirely for users who prefer reduced motion.

Final result

A fast, dependency-free, three-page static site: home, this case study, and a custom 404. Every claim on it is true, every link works, and adding a future project means copying one HTML block and one page.

What I learned

Constraints written down early save rework later — the "no invention" rule shaped every section. And a clear brief is a skill in itself: the better the spec, the less correction the build needed.
